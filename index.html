The approach

The original guide's main point: S3 tests reasoning and approximation, not recall of content. Learn only the content that makes you faster in the exam, and start doing questions early.

Chemistry is your time bank

You already have the chemistry. Your goal there is to be fast, so you can bank minutes on chem stems and spend them on physics and unfamiliar bio graphs. Skim Jesse's chem videos to see how GAMSAT frames the material, but don't relearn it.

Physics and maths come first

Physics only needs to be at high-school level, but you need to know the core formulae well and be quick at algebra. You can't use a calculator, so logs, scientific notation and rearranging equations need to become automatic.

Bio is mostly reading

Biology questions are mostly graph reading and pulling the relevant facts out of wordy stems. Jesse's bio crash course plus plenty of practice questions is enough. Don't over-study the content.

Start with Jesse Osbourne

The author called Jesse their "goat". In this plan his videos are your first stop for every topic, and Khan Academy is only for filling gaps when a Jesse video feels too advanced.

The method: take paper notes on every video (colours, highlighters, whatever keeps you engaged). Pause and work each practice question with him before he solves it. If a video loses you, go to Khan Academy or Google for that concept, then come back and rewatch until the video no longer confuses you.

Order: Maths Skills → Exam Skills → Physics crash course → Chem (quick skim) and Bio crash courses → Sample questions + walkthroughs.

Review system

The guide called past ACER papers "the backbone" of their study: aim for quality over quantity, and review every question thoroughly. "Some days I'd spend 2 hours tearing my hair reviewing a single stem."

1 · Rate your confidence before you check

Mark each answer as high or low confidence, then mark it right or wrong.

Known gapName the gap and add it to next week's study list.

2 · Name the error

  • Knowledge gap: rewatch the Jesse video, then backfill with Khan Academy
  • Missed keyword: underline every qualifier in the stem next time
  • Misunderstood stem: rewrite the question in your own words before answering
  • Calculation error: add the operation to your daily maths drill
  • Too slow: practise estimating; round harder and check the answer options first

3 · Tag the skill

Label each question by what it tests, e.g. bio · unfamiliar graph chem · pH large-number calc phys · rearrange + units ochem · H-counting. Over time you'll start spotting these in the exam: "Most questions are asking very little of you other than identifying what they're asking for and what skills to use."

Building up stamina

Untimed 10-question blocks → timed 10-question blocks → untimed half paper → timed half paper → full timed paper under exam conditions. The author scored about 30% on their first untimed diagnostic. A low starting score is normal.

The original guide in brief

Written by a graphic design graduate who hadn't studied chemistry or physics since Year 10. With 4 months of prep they scored 85 in S3 and 75 overall in the March 2026 GAMSAT.

Their 4-month timeline

  • Month 1 · Basics. Worked through the whole Khan Academy chemistry course. Not efficient, but it kept them from giving up. (You can skip this: your background already covers it.)
  • Month 2 · Moving to skills. Did badly on the first practice test, and the first Jesse video went over their head. Studied one discipline at a time, sometimes spending a whole day on one stem.
  • Month 3 · Drilling. Did lots of questions and relearned whatever slowed them down (acid–base came up repeatedly). Started watching the clock and practised estimating.
  • Month 4 · Sharpening. Did full timed exams and cut anything that wasn't worth practising.

How this plan differs

  • You have ~5 months instead of 4, so there's room for a slower physics rebuild and a lighter Christmas period.
  • Their Month 1 on chemistry becomes your Weeks 1–5 on maths and physics.
  • Jesse's videos come first for every topic, with Khan Academy as the fallback, not the other way round.
  • Question practice starts in Week 5, earlier than the original's Month 2.
